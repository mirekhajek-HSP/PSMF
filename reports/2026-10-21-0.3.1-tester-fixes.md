## Gate 1 — typing, and the keyboard over a low field (points 1 and 2)

### What changed for a referee

- **Typing lands where the cursor is**, in every field of the app, dialogs
  included. "Marek" is "Marek". It does not depend on how fast the app answers.
- **Tap a field low on the screen and the keyboard stays.** The screen moves up
  so that the field is above the keyboard, with room under it for the button
  that belongs with it (one touch target and a gap, 68 dp).
- **What still takes the keyboard away is unchanged**: a tap on anything that
  is not a field, leaving the step, the keyboard's own Done, and now only a
  *finger* dragging the content. The screen scrolling itself no longer counts.

### Why it happened

**The cursor.** Every field in the app is given its text by a ViewModel's
`StateFlow` — the lineup rows as much as the dialogs — so a field is told what
it asked for one frame after it asked. Material's `String` text field keeps its
own `TextFieldValue`, and each time it is recomposed with a text that differs
from its own it fits its cursor to the text it was given *and writes that back*.
After "M", the field recomposed once with the old empty text; the cursor was
fitted to 0 and written back; "M" arrived, with the cursor in front of it; "arek"
went in front of that. Reproduced on the JVM with a screen that hands its state
back a frame late. *Why the testers saw it in the dialogs and not elsewhere I
could not establish from the code* — every field takes the same route. The
dialogs sit in a window of their own and may take a frame longer.

**The keyboard.** `DismissOnScroll` took every `NestedScrollSource.UserInput`
scroll for the referee's finger, on the belief (written into its comment) that a
list scrolling a focused field into view is not `UserInput`. It is. A throwaway
probe on a list with a low field, shrunk by 400 dp the way the keyboard shrinks
it, logged the foundation's own scroll arriving at a parent as
`UserInput Offset(0.0, 0.0)`, then `UserInput Offset(0.0, -24.4)`, `-42.4`, … —
fourteen calls, all `UserInput`. The probe is deleted. The keyboard was asked to
go the moment it came.

### What was done

- `PsmfTextField` holds the `TextFieldValue` itself (saved the way Material's
  own is, with `TextFieldValue.Saver`). The text on show is always the screen's; the cursor and the
  composition are the referee's; the one is never written over the other.
- `KeyboardDismissal`'s pointer handler now tells the nested-scroll connection
  when a finger has moved past touch slop since it last touched down; a scroll
  takes the keyboard only if it is `UserInput` **and** that is so. It reads the
  move in the *first* pass, before the list sees it, and keeps the fact until
  the next touch.
- When the area a screen lives in gets shorter while a field has the focus,
  `KeyboardDismissal` asks the field to be brought into view with
  `PsmfDimens.minTouchTarget + itemSpacing` below it. Fields report their
  focus and carry a `BringIntoViewRequester` for this.

### How it was proved

New tests, written first, run against HEAD's two sources (`PsmfTextField.kt`,
`KeyboardDismissal.kt` put back as they were): **5 of the 9 failed there, all by
assertion**.

| Test (new) | At HEAD |
|---|---|
| `typingMarekGivesMarekWhenTheStateComesBackAFrameLate` | `expected:<[Marek]> but was:<[arekM]>` |
| `typingMarekGivesMarekWhenTheStateTakesTwoFramesToCome` | `expected:<[Marek]> but was:<[arekM]>` |
| `aFieldTheListScrollsIntoViewKeepsItsFocus` | `Failed to assert the following: (Focused = 'true')` — the field, `Focused = 'false'` |
| `theRoomForTheButtonUnderALowFieldComesUpWithItAboveTheKeyboard` | the same: the focus was already gone. With the dismissal fixed and no room: `no room under the field: its bottom 368.0, the list's 368.0` |
| `aFingerDraggingTheSameListStillTakesTheFocusAway` | the same `Focused = 'false'`, at the step before the swipe |

Guards that **pass at HEAD and must go on passing**: `aSingleQuickFlickTakesTheFocusAwayToo`,
`typingMarekGivesMarekWhenTheStateIsChangedAtOnce`,
`aChangeTheScreenRefusesIsNotLeftOnShow`, `aTextTheScreenSetsItselfReplacesWhatWasTyped`.
Every existing `KeyboardTest` still passes (12 before; 16 now).

The scroll tests drive a list with a low field and make the keyboard come by
growing the bottom padding of the area (the host has no software keyboard;
`imePadding` does exactly this on a phone), after the field has taken focus.
What they see of the keyboard is what the rest of `KeyboardTest` sees: the
focus, and whether the keyboard was asked to `hide()`.

**Each new behaviour broken once.**

| Broken | Caught by |
|---|---|
| HEAD's own behaviour: any `UserInput` scroll takes the keyboard | the three scroll tests above (they are among the 5 failures) |
| `PsmfTextField` shows the typed record, not the screen's text | `aChangeTheScreenRefusesIsNotLeftOnShow`, `aTextTheScreenSetsItselfReplacesWhatWasTyped` |
| the pointer handler never says a finger is dragging | `startingToScrollTakesTheFocusAway` (existing), `aFingerDraggingTheSameListStillTakesTheFocusAway`, `aSingleQuickFlickTakesTheFocusAwayToo` |
| the room below is never asked for | `theRoomForTheButtonUnderALowFieldComesUpWithItAboveTheKeyboard` |
| a field never reports its focus | the same test |
| **the finger is noticed after the list has handled the move, not before** | **nothing.** The host passes a drag's distance to the list after the pointer event, so it cannot tell the first pass from the last. The first-pass read is kept because a single fast flick may be dispatched inside the move on a phone; that is a choice, not a proof. |

`aSingleQuickFlickTakesTheFocusAwayToo` earned its place early: the first
version of the finger flag was cleared when the finger lifted, and this test —
one move, then up — failed on that, because the list passes the distance on
after the event. The flag is now cleared at the next touch instead.

Green: `./gradlew build :shared:allTests detekt`. `ktlint -F` run on the four
files first, since they were written through the UNC path; no file mode changed.

### What the JVM could not show

- **The keyboard.** Only that the app did not ask it to go and did not drop the
  focus, which is what closes it.
- **What the IME does with one frame of stale text.** The field shows the old
  text for a frame; the cursor is no longer lost, but the IME is told "" and then
  "M". Gboard and the iOS keyboard are the judges.
- **Insets.** The Android emulator measures `imePadding` for real, and the
  prompt forbids it here. The 68 dp of room is argued from the lineup, whose
  lowest row has the *Přidat hráče* button right under it.
- **iOS.** Nothing here was built for it. How the iOS side makes room for the
  keyboard I have not read; whether the area *shrinks* (which is what asks for
  the room) or the content moves some other way is for the iPhone to say.

### Judgement calls

All three are in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, 2026-10-21 "0.3.1, Gate 1", each with a
"Reverses if".

1. **The cursor is the referee's, the text is the screen's.**
2. **A scroll is the referee's only if a finger moved it**, with the fact kept
   until the next touch.
3. **Room for the control under the field**, 68 dp, asked for when the area
   shrinks. The brief's outcome says "the field and the button under it"; the
   foundation alone gives only the field.

### Not done

- **A state that arrives after the next keystroke** still loses a character,
  as before. That would need the typed text to stay the truth until the screen
  answers, and a way to tell a late answer from a refusal. One frame, which is
  what the brief asked for, and two, which the test covers, work.
- **Moving on with *Next* does not ask for the room under the new field**; the
  foundation brings it just into view, as before. Only the keyboard arriving
  does. That is the only case missed; I did not check which forms have a
  control under a field they move on to.
- **No emulator, no phone, no iOS.** The UNPROVEN in the table is the keyboard
  itself.
- `docs/DECISIONS.md` has the **2026-10-21 entry from the brief at line ~407,
  between the 17 October entries**, not at the top. I did not move it; this
  gate's entry is at the top. (Gate 7 can put it in order.)
- `reports/README.md`'s index stops at 2026-10-08. Left for Gate 7.

### For the phone checklist (Gate 7 assembles it)

Points 1 and 2 are first, on a real phone, installed over 0.3.0 without
uninstalling.

1. **A dialog.** Sestavy → *Přidat hráče*. Tap *Jméno*, type "Marek" slowly, then
   clear it and type it as fast as you can. **Should:** Marek, both times.
   **Failure:** "arekM", or any letter in front of an earlier one. Do the same in
   *Příjmení*, and with a number in the card field.
2. **The cursor stays where you put it.** Type "Novk", tap between the v and the
   k, type "á". **Should:** "Novák". **Failure:** the á at the front or the end.
3. **The lowest row.** A lineup of 15 or more; mark the last player, tap the
   shirt-number field. **Should:** the keyboard stays up, the row moves up above
   it, and the *Přidat hráče* button is above the keyboard too.
   **Failure:** the keyboard flashes and goes; or the field is flush against
   the keyboard with the button hidden behind it.
4. **A finger still takes it away.** With the keyboard up, drag the list slowly,
   then flick it quickly. **Should:** the keyboard goes both times.
5. **The rest is as it was.** A tap on empty space or on a button takes the keyboard
   away. *Zpět* and leaving the step do too. The last field of a form says Done.
   A tap on the same field again leaves the keyboard up.

**On the iPhone:** the number pad has no return key, so the same steps 1–5 with
the number fields — shirt number, *Číslo RP*, date of birth — are where this
bites; and the dialog in particular, which is a window of its own. If the
screen does not make room under a low field, say so: the 68 dp depends on the
area shrinking, which iOS may do differently.

## Gate 2 — compact lineup rows (point 3)

### What changed for a referee

- **A player who has not come is one line**: the mark and the name, a touch
  target (56 dp) tall, and a hairline. Twenty of them, with nobody marked, are
  1,140 dp where they were about 3,300. With eight marked, the squad in the
  renders below is about 1,900 dp (read off the picture, not measured). A name
  that carries a suspension badge is as tall as the name and its badge need, as
  before.
- **Marking opens the row downwards**: the shirt number beside the name, *Číslo
  RP* and *Nemá průkaz* under it, as in 0.3.0. **The name that was tapped does
  not move**; the names below go down. Taking the mark back closes it again.
- **It opens at once**, with no animation (see judgement call 4).
- **The names now touch.** There is no gap between two rows, only the hairline,
  so every stretch of the list is a name to tap.
- **What was typed comes back.** Unmark a player and mark them again on the same
  visit: the shirt number, the number from the card (even half of it) and the
  date of birth are in the row when it reopens.

### Why it was tall

`SquadRow` laid every row out as an open one and then hid the fields (`alpha(0)`,
out of the accessibility tree) so that marking moved nothing: 160 dp of blank
per unmarked player, plus the list's own 12 dp between items. Prompt 13's "rows
do not jump under the thumb" taken further than meant (DECISIONS 2026-10-21).

### What was done

- `SquadRow`: the shirt-number field and the identification line are composed
  only for a marked row. The first line is top-aligned (so the name is where it
  was whether or not a field beside it is there). `hiddenUnless`, the `enabled`
  parameters and the alpha are gone.
- `LineupScreen`: the list no longer spaces its items; the items around the
  squad keep their 12 dp with `Spaced`, and one `Spacer` sits between the last
  row and the advisory note. The pinned count has its own padding and is
  followed directly by the first row.

### How it was proved

**Written first, against HEAD's sources.** Of the five `SquadRowTest` tests
that existed then, **four failed there, all by assertion**; the fifth is a
guard that passes at HEAD and must go on passing.

### What the JVM could not show

- **How rows that touch feel under a thumb.** Each name is 56 dp and the only
  boundary is a hairline. Whether a tap near the boundary lands on the name the
  referee meant is for a thumb.
- **The name against its field.** Top-aligned, the name sits about 4 dp above
  the middle of the *Dres* field at the standard font (12 dp at 1.5). In the
  renders it reads as aligned to the field's top edge, not wrong; a phone is the
  judge.
- **The keyboard with an opened row.** Gate 1's room under a focused field was
  proved on a list with a low field, not on this one. A marked row near the foot
  of the lineup is checklist step 6.
- **iOS.** Common code, not built for it.

### Judgement calls

All four are in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, 2026-10-21 "0.3.1, Gate 2", each with a
"Reverses if".

1. **The fields are not drawn while a row is closed.** What was typed lives in
   the state the fields are drawn from, which keeps it.
2. **The rows touch**: the list's 12 dp gap between items is removed for the
   squad, kept around it. Not asked for in the brief; a strip a thumb falls
   into is not a touch target, and it was 12 of every 69 dp.
3. **The first line is top-aligned**, not centred, so the name does not move.
4. **No animation.** I built one (the row's height over 150 ms) and took it
   out. A tap on the next name made while the row above was opening was lost in
   the JVM harness. With the clock frozen 60 ms into the opening, a tap on the
   row that was opening (it does not move) registered, and a tap on the name
   below it, which had moved 15 dp, did not; with nothing animating, the same
   tap on that name worked. The likely mechanism is that a tap whose release
   lands outside its target is cancelled; I did not read the framework to
   confirm it. The brief allowed the animation only "if a tap never waits for
   it", and I could not show that a tap on a moving row is not lost, so rows
   open at once.

### Not done

- **A frame after the first one, the next name shifts by a further 2 dp** (the
  new fields are measured twice, or a font arrives; I did not trace it). It is
  still after two frames, which is what the at-once test checks.
- **Dark theme** for the new rows was not rendered; nothing in them is
  colour-specific beyond what the 0.3.0 row used.
- **Nothing on a phone**, as the prompt says; the checklist below is the proof
  that is owed.

### For the phone checklist (Gate 7 assembles it)

1. **A whole squad.** Sestavy with a team of 15–25, nobody marked. **Should:**
   every name is one line, and the squad is a screen and a half or so, not
   three or four. **Failure:** blank space under the names.
2. **The name stays.** Tap a name in the middle. **Should:** that name does not
   move; *Dres* appears beside it and *Číslo RP* with *Nemá průkaz* under it; the
   names below go down. **Failure:** the name jumps, or the row opens above it.
3. **It closes again.** Tap the same name. **Should:** the row closes, the rows
   below come back, the name has still not moved.
4. **What was typed comes back.** In a row type a shirt number and half a card
   number; take the mark back, give it again. **Should:** both are there.
   **Failure:** the fields are empty.
5. **Working down the list with a thumb.** Mark six players one after another,
   without looking for the next name. **Should:** each tap marks the name under
   the thumb. **Failure:** a tap marks the neighbour, or goes nowhere (the rows
   touch; this is the one the JVM cannot answer).
6. **The lowest row, with the keyboard.** Mark the last player and tap *Dres*.
   **Should:** the keyboard stays and the row and *Přidat hráče* are above it
   (Gate 1, step 3, now with the new rows).
7. **A large system font** (150 %): steps 1–3 again. **Should:** the same, the
   closed rows still no taller than a touch target. **Failure:** names wrap and
   push their rows apart, or the name moves when marked.
8. **A player with the suspension badge.** **Should:** the badge shows whether
   or not they are marked, and the closed row is as tall as the name and badge.

**On the iPhone:** the same eight steps; nothing known to differ.
